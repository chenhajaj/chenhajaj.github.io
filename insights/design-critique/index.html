Design education, October 2026

How a design critique sounds

Architecture and design students learn largely through the crit: a student presents, a tutor responds, and ideas move back and forth. Crits are central to the studio, but they are often tutor-centred and ambiguous, and a negative tone can make students hold back. Until now they were assessed by the design that came out, not by the conversation itself.

We transcribe crits and use natural language processing to follow two things through time: the sentiment of the exchange, and every first occurrence, the moment a new design concept enters the conversation and who brought it. In crits with computer science and architecture students, more of the new ideas came from the students when the tone was positive.

We then compared crits held in immersive virtual reality, where the design is shown at full scale, with crits on ordinary screens. VR crits produced a higher rate of new concepts, and the link between tone and new ideas differed between the two media. Measured this way, a crit can give the tutor feedback while it is still happening.
