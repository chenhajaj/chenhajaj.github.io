Explainers

Our research in plain words, for partners, journalists and future students outside the field. Each explainer says what our papers mean in practice and points to the research behind them.

  • Encrypted traffic, October 2026

    Saying "I don't know" has a price

    A traffic classifier meets apps it was never trained on. It can refuse to label what looks unfamiliar, but every refusal threshold is a trade. Our study shows that moving the rejection rate from 10% to 30% catches up to 50% more unknown traffic while false alarms grow by less than 10%.
  • Encrypted traffic, October 2026

    Post-quantum encryption leaves a fingerprint

    The internet is starting to switch to encryption that quantum computers cannot break. The new keys are much larger, and that changes the shape of the traffic. From encrypted traffic alone, our PQClass pipeline tells whether a connection uses post-quantum cryptography, and which browser and operating system made it.
  • Encrypted traffic, October 2026

    When the test changes the winner

    Papers on traffic classification usually credit their gains to a newer model. We trained six deep models 2,016 times and found that how the data is split into training and test sets decides which model wins. Split it properly and the leader falls to last place.
  • Design education, October 2026

    How a design critique sounds

    In the design studio, students learn in critiques: short conversations with a tutor about their work. With Hadas Sopher and John Gero, we use language models to track the tone of each critique and the moments a new design idea appears. Students introduce more of their own ideas when the tone is positive, and more new ideas appear in virtual reality.
  • E-commerce and multimodal AI, October 2026

    Recommending a product nobody has clicked yet

    Recommendation engines learn from clicks and purchases, so a brand-new product has nothing to learn from. We describe a new item by its photo, its text and its attributes, find its closest existing look-alike, and borrow that item's history. On fashion data this improved hit rate by 7% for new items, with no retraining.
  • Adversarial AI, October 2026

    CAPTCHAs people can read and bots can't

    Image classifiers now solve CAPTCHAs, so designers add adversarial noise that fools the bots. The trouble is that the noise also bothers people and can be filtered out. Our method chooses a smarter wrong answer for the bot and spends noise only where it counts: 20–40% less noise, fewer steps, and harder to filter.
  • Encrypted traffic, September 2026

    New classes in seconds, without retraining

    Most traffic classifiers become expensive to maintain when applications and threats change. Classification by retrieval stores compact flow representations and learns a new class by adding a few labeled examples to the index. The same nearest-neighbor distance also helps detect traffic that does not belong to any known class.
  • Production ML, August 2026

    Accuracy is only one part of a deployable classifier

    Real network systems also care about update time, memory, latency, privacy, and behavior on unknown traffic. Our work measures those trade-offs explicitly so a model can be chosen for the operating environment, not only for a benchmark leaderboard.
  • Trustworthy AI, July 2026

    Security and clinical ML share a hard problem

    Both domains change after deployment, contain rare high-cost cases, and punish confident mistakes. That makes robustness, out-of-distribution detection, interpretable evidence, and careful validation common design requirements across our cyber and health projects.
